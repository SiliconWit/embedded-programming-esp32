A plant dries out while you are away, and the sensor beside it knew for two days. The web server you built for the thermostat cannot help: it answers only when somebody asks, and only inside the local network. A device that must report by itself, to people who are somewhere else, needs a different method. MQTT is that method: the device sends each reading to a server on the internet, and the server passes it to everyone who asked for it. In this lesson you send soil moisture readings and a low-moisture alert through a free MQTT server, encrypted, and you receive settings back. You also learn what "sent" does and does not promise. #ESP32 #MQTT #IoT

## Circuit Connections

<InArticleAd />


Connect the capacitive soil moisture sensor to the ESP32 on a breadboard:

| ESP32 Pin | Component | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| GPIO 36 (VP) | Sensor analog output (AOUT) | ADC1_CHANNEL_0 |
| 3.3V | Sensor VCC | Power supply |
| GND | Sensor GND | Common ground |

The sensor has three pins: VCC, GND and AOUT (analog output). Push it into the soil up to the line marked on the board, and keep the electronics above that line dry. The output is about 1.2 V in wet soil and about 2.8 V in dry air. You measure the exact values for your sensor later.

The ESP32 measures this voltage with an ADC (analog to digital converter), which turns a voltage into a number. GPIO 36 is an input-only pin on ADC1. Use ADC1: the other converter, ADC2, is shared with the Wi-Fi radio and gives bad readings while Wi-Fi is on.

## MQTT Protocol Overview

<InArticleAd />


With HTTP, one side asks and the other answers. A sensor in a garden has nobody to ask it, and it sits behind a home router that lets no requests in from the internet. MQTT turns the direction round: the device opens the connection outward, to a server everyone can reach, and sends readings when it has them. The messages are small, and the protocol is built for links that drop. It runs over TCP, on port 1883 without encryption and on port 8883 with TLS.

### Publish/Subscribe Model

MQTT devices never talk to each other directly. Each one connects to the server, named the broker. A device that has data publishes a message under a label, the topic. A device that wants that data subscribes to the topic. The broker passes each message to every subscriber of its topic.

A broker works like a notice board with named sections: a sender pins a note in a section and leaves, not knowing who reads it, or whether anybody does. The comparison stops here: a note stays on a board, but a broker normally passes a message on at once and keeps no copy, so a later reader sees nothing. (Retained messages and persistent sessions, below, change this.)

```text
 ┌──────────┐  publish                 ┌──────────┐
 │  ESP32   │  home/garden/moisture    │  Phone   │
 │  sensor  ├───────────┐        ┌────>│  app     │
 └──────────┘           v        │     └──────────┘
                     ┌──────────┐│  subscribed to
                     │  Broker  ├┤  home/garden/#
                     └──────────┘│     ┌──────────┐
                                 └────>│  Laptop  │
                                       │  script  │
                                       └──────────┘
```

A topic is a text with levels separated by forward slashes:

```
home/garden/moisture       (soil moisture percentage)
home/garden/alert          (low moisture warning)
home/garden/config         (remote configuration commands)
home/garden/status         (online/offline status)
```

A subscriber can use wildcards: `+` matches a single level (`home/+/moisture` matches `home/garden/moisture` and `home/balcony/moisture`), while `#` matches all remaining levels (`home/#` matches everything under `home/`).

### Quality of Service (QoS) Levels

A network can lose a message. The quality of service (QoS) says how much effort is made to deliver one. The sender chooses it for each message:

- QoS 0, at most once. The sender sends the message and forgets it. If it is lost, nobody knows. Use it for frequent readings where one missing value does not matter.
- QoS 1, at least once. The sender keeps the message until the receiver confirms it (PUBACK), and sends it again if no confirmation comes. The message arrives, but it may arrive twice.
- QoS 2, exactly once. Four packets (PUBLISH, PUBREC, PUBREL, PUBCOMP) make sure that the message arrives once and is not repeated. It is the slowest.

One point is often misunderstood: QoS covers one step of the journey, device to broker or broker to one subscriber, not device to phone. A device that publishes with QoS 2 and gets the confirmation knows that the broker has the message, and nothing about the phone. Each subscriber chooses its own QoS when it subscribes, and a subscriber that is not connected receives nothing, unless it has a persistent session. **Published does not mean received.**

The monitor uses QoS 1 for readings, because a repeated reading does no harm, and QoS 2 for alerts, so that the broker does not get the same alert twice.

### Retained Messages

A message published with the retain flag is kept by the broker as the last value of its topic. Each new subscriber gets it at once, even if it was sent hours ago. This suits a status topic: a new subscriber learns immediately whether the sensor is online.

### Clean Sessions vs Persistent Sessions

With `clean_session = true`, the broker forgets a client when it disconnects: its subscriptions, and any messages that arrive for it meanwhile.

With `clean_session = false`, the broker keeps the client's subscriptions, and stores the QoS 1 and QoS 2 messages that arrive while the client is away. The client receives them when it returns. For this the client needs a fixed client ID, so that the broker recognises it.

### Last Will and Testament (LWT)

A device that loses power cannot say goodbye, so it leaves its goodbye with the broker in advance: when it connects, it gives the broker a "last will" message. If it later disappears without a proper disconnect (power loss, crash, network failure), the broker publishes that message for it, and the subscribers know the device is gone.

## ESP-MQTT Client Basics

<InArticleAd />


ESP-IDF includes an MQTT client, ESP-MQTT (the `mqtt` component, header `mqtt_client.h`). It implements MQTT 3.1.1 with all three QoS levels, TLS, persistent sessions and the last will. Like the Wi-Fi driver, it runs in its own task and reports to you through events.

### Client Configuration

One structure, `esp_mqtt_client_config_t`, holds everything about the connection. The address, username and password below are placeholders. In the complete firmware they come from menuconfig and are never written in the source code:

```c
#include "mqtt_client.h"

extern const uint8_t server_cert_pem_start[] asm("_binary_hivemq_server_cert_pem_start");
extern const uint8_t server_cert_pem_end[]   asm("_binary_hivemq_server_cert_pem_end");

esp_mqtt_client_config_t mqtt_cfg = {
    .broker = {
        .address = {
            .uri = "mqtts://xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx.s1.eu.hivemq.cloud:8883",
        },
        .verification = {
            .certificate = (const char *)server_cert_pem_start,
        },
    },
    .credentials = {
        .username = "your_username",
        .authentication = {
            .password = "your_password",
        },
        .client_id = "esp32-soil-monitor",
    },
    .session = {
        .last_will = {
            .topic = "home/garden/status",
            .msg = "sensor-offline",
            .msg_len = 14,
            .qos = 1,
            .retain = 1,
        },
    },
};
```

The `mqtts://` at the start of the `uri` selects TLS. The `certificate` field points to a certificate stored inside the firmware, which the TLS section explains. The `last_will` fields are the message that the broker publishes if the ESP32 disappears.

### Event Handler

One handler function receives every MQTT event:

```c
static void mqtt_event_handler(void *handler_args, esp_event_base_t base,
                                 int32_t event_id, void *event_data)
{
    esp_mqtt_event_handle_t event = event_data;
    esp_mqtt_client_handle_t client = event->client;

    switch ((esp_mqtt_event_id_t)event_id) {
        case MQTT_EVENT_CONNECTED:
            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "MQTT connected to broker");
            /* Subscribe to the config topic after connecting */
            esp_mqtt_client_subscribe(client, "home/garden/config", 1);
            /* Publish online status with retain flag */
            esp_mqtt_client_publish(client, "home/garden/status",
                                     "sensor-online", 0, 1, 1);
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_DISCONNECTED:
            ESP_LOGW(TAG, "MQTT disconnected, will auto-reconnect");
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_SUBSCRIBED:
            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Subscribed, msg_id=%d", event->msg_id);
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_DATA:
            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Received data on topic=%.*s",
                     event->topic_len, event->topic);
            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Payload=%.*s",
                     event->data_len, event->data);
            /* Process incoming commands here */
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_ERROR:
            ESP_LOGE(TAG, "MQTT error type: %d",
                     event->error_handle->error_type);
            if (event->error_handle->error_type ==
                MQTT_ERROR_TYPE_TCP_TRANSPORT) {
                ESP_LOGE(TAG, "TLS error: 0x%04x",
                         event->error_handle->esp_tls_last_esp_err);
            }
            break;

        default:
            break;
    }
}
```

### Starting the Client

Subscribe inside `MQTT_EVENT_CONNECTED`, not once at start: this event comes again after every reconnection, and a broker that has forgotten the session has also forgotten the subscription. Then create and start the client:

```c
esp_mqtt_client_handle_t client = esp_mqtt_client_init(&mqtt_cfg);
esp_mqtt_client_register_event(client, ESP_EVENT_ANY_ID,
                                 mqtt_event_handler, NULL);
esp_mqtt_client_start(client);
```

`esp_mqtt_client_start()` creates a task that manages the connection, the TLS setup and the keep-alive (a small packet sent at intervals to show that the client is still there). If the connection drops, the client tries again by itself, every 10 seconds by default (`.network.reconnect_timeout_ms`).

## TLS Configuration

<InArticleAd />


Without encryption, anyone on the path between the device and the broker can read the readings, read the password, and send commands in your name. TLS prevents this, and HiveMQ Cloud requires it.

TLS also answers a second question: is this server really the broker, or somebody pretending? The broker proves its identity with a certificate, as a traveller does with a passport: a passport is trusted because a known authority issued it, a certificate because a certificate authority (CA) signed it. The ESP32 carries the CA's own certificate, the root CA certificate, to check the signature. This certificate is public: not a secret and not a password.

```text
 ┌──────────┐          ┌───────────────┐
 │  ESP32   │          │  MQTT Broker  │
 │          │  TCP     │  (HiveMQ)     │
 │  mbedTLS ├─────────>│  Port 8883    │
 │  Client  │  TLS 1.2 │               │
 │          │<─────────┤  Root CA:     │
 │  CA cert │  Verify  │  ISRG X1      │
 │ embedded │  chain   │               │
 └──────────┘          └───────────────┘
   Firmware embeds           Broker presents
   root CA PEM file          server certificate
```

### Obtaining the Root CA Certificate

HiveMQ Cloud's certificate is signed by the authority ISRG Root X1, the CA of Let's Encrypt. Put its root certificate into the firmware:

<Steps>
1. Download the ISRG Root X1 certificate in PEM format from [letsencrypt.org/certs/isrgrootx1.pem](https://letsencrypt.org/certs/isrgrootx1.pem). Alternatively, you can export it from your browser by visiting the broker URL and inspecting the certificate chain.

2. Save the file as `main/hivemq_server_cert.pem` in your project directory.

3. Tell the build system to embed the certificate by adding it to `CMakeLists.txt`:
   ```cmake
   idf_component_register(SRCS "main.c"
                          INCLUDE_DIRS "."
                          EMBED_TXTFILES "hivemq_server_cert.pem")
   ```

4. Reference the embedded certificate in your code using the linker symbols:
   ```c
   extern const uint8_t server_cert_pem_start[]
       asm("_binary_hivemq_server_cert_pem_start");
   extern const uint8_t server_cert_pem_end[]
       asm("_binary_hivemq_server_cert_pem_end");
   ```
</Steps>

`EMBED_TXTFILES` copies the file into the firmware, and the two symbols mark where it begins and ends. At run time, `server_cert_pem_start` points to the first byte of the certificate.

### What Happens During the TLS Handshake

The handshake is the exchange at the start of a TLS connection:

1. The ESP32 says hello to the broker.
2. The broker sends its certificate.
3. The ESP32 checks the signature on it with the root CA certificate in its firmware.
4. If the check succeeds, the two sides agree on a secret key for this session.
5. All MQTT traffic after that is encrypted with the key.

If the check fails (another CA, an expired certificate, a wrong host name), the connection is refused and the handler receives `MQTT_EVENT_ERROR`. The `esp_tls_last_esp_err` field holds the TLS error code.

## Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensor

<InArticleAd />


The sensor is a capacitor whose plates are two copper areas on its board, with the soil around them as the material between the plates. Water raises the capacitance far more than dry soil does. (Its dielectric constant, the measure of this effect, is about 80, against about 4 for dry soil.) A circuit on the sensor turns the capacitance into a voltage.

### Capacitive vs Resistive Sensors

Older sensors pass a current through the soil between two bare metal probes, which corrode, so after some weeks the readings drift. A capacitive sensor has no bare metal in the soil (its plates are under a waterproof coating), so it lasts outdoors.

### Reading the ADC

The raw number from the ADC differs a little from chip to chip. The driver can correct it with calibration data stored in each chip at the factory and give you millivolts. "Oneshot" means one reading for each call:

```c
#include "esp_adc/adc_oneshot.h"
#include "esp_adc/adc_cali.h"
#include "esp_adc/adc_cali_scheme.h"

static adc_oneshot_unit_handle_t adc_handle;
static adc_cali_handle_t cali_handle;

static void adc_init(void)
{
    /* Configure ADC1 unit */
    adc_oneshot_unit_init_cfg_t unit_cfg = {
        .unit_id = ADC_UNIT_1,
    };
    adc_oneshot_new_unit(&unit_cfg, &adc_handle);

    /* Configure channel 0 (GPIO 36) with 12 dB attenuation for
     * the widest input range */
    adc_oneshot_chan_cfg_t chan_cfg = {
        .atten = ADC_ATTEN_DB_12,
        .bitwidth = ADC_BITWIDTH_12,
    };
    adc_oneshot_config_channel(adc_handle, ADC_CHANNEL_0, &chan_cfg);

    /* Set up calibration for voltage conversion */
#if ADC_CALI_SCHEME_CURVE_FITTING_SUPPORTED
    adc_cali_curve_fitting_config_t cali_cfg = {
        .unit_id = ADC_UNIT_1,
        .chan = ADC_CHANNEL_0,
        .atten = ADC_ATTEN_DB_12,
        .bitwidth = ADC_BITWIDTH_12,
    };
    adc_cali_create_scheme_curve_fitting(&cali_cfg, &cali_handle);
#elif ADC_CALI_SCHEME_LINE_FITTING_SUPPORTED
    adc_cali_line_fitting_config_t cali_cfg = {
        .unit_id = ADC_UNIT_1,
        .atten = ADC_ATTEN_DB_12,
        .bitwidth = ADC_BITWIDTH_12,
    };
    adc_cali_create_scheme_line_fitting(&cali_cfg, &cali_handle);
#endif
}

static int read_soil_moisture_mv(void)
{
    int raw = 0;
    int voltage_mv = 0;

    adc_oneshot_read(adc_handle, ADC_CHANNEL_0, &raw);
    adc_cali_raw_to_voltage(cali_handle, raw, &voltage_mv);

    return voltage_mv;
}
```

Attenuation scales the input voltage down before the ADC measures it, so a higher voltage fits its range. The 12 dB setting (`ADC_ATTEN_DB_12`, `ADC_ATTEN_DB_11` in older ESP-IDF releases) gives the widest range: specified for about 150 mV to 2450 mV, and readable, less accurately, up to about 3.1 V, which covers most of these sensors.

### Calibrating Wet and Dry Values

Each sensor gives slightly different voltages, so measure the two end points of yours:

<Steps>
1. Hold the sensor in open air (completely dry) and read the ADC voltage. This is your `DRY_VALUE_MV`. A typical value is 2700 mV.

2. Insert the sensor into a cup of water up to the marked line (do not submerge the electronics). Read the ADC voltage. This is your `WET_VALUE_MV`. A typical value is 1200 mV.

3. Use these values to convert millivolts to a 0-100% moisture percentage:
   ```c
   #define DRY_VALUE_MV  2700
   #define WET_VALUE_MV  1200

   static int voltage_to_moisture_pct(int voltage_mv)
   {
       if (voltage_mv >= DRY_VALUE_MV) return 0;
       if (voltage_mv <= WET_VALUE_MV) return 100;

       int pct = (DRY_VALUE_MV - voltage_mv) * 100
                 / (DRY_VALUE_MV - WET_VALUE_MV);
       return pct;
   }
   ```
</Steps>

The voltage falls as the soil gets wetter, so the formula measures how far the voltage is below the dry value, as a fraction of the whole range. With these values, a reading of 1770 mV is 930 mV below the dry value, and the whole range is 1500 mV: 930 × 100 / 1500 = 62 percent.

This percentage is a position between your two end points. It is not the water content of the soil in percent.

One ADC reading can differ from the next by tens of millivolts, and 15 mV is already one percent here (1500 mV for 100 percent). For a steadier value, take 16 readings in a row and publish their average. Note also that the dry value of 2700 mV lies above 2450 mV, where the ADC is less accurate: the 0 percent end is the least certain one.

## Publishing Sensor Data

<InArticleAd />


The payload is the content of a message. MQTT does not care what is in it. JSON is a good choice, because every subscriber can read it.

### JSON Payload Formatting

For a payload this small, `snprintf` is enough and needs less memory than the cJSON library:

```c
static void publish_moisture(esp_mqtt_client_handle_t client,
                              int moisture_pct, int voltage_mv)
{
    char payload[128];
    snprintf(payload, sizeof(payload),
             "{\"moisture\":%d,\"voltage_mv\":%d,\"uptime\":%lld}",
             moisture_pct, voltage_mv,
             (long long)(esp_timer_get_time() / 1000000));

    int msg_id = esp_mqtt_client_enqueue(client,
                                          "home/garden/moisture",
                                          payload, 0,
                                          1,      /* QoS 1 */
                                          0,      /* No retain */
                                          true);  /* Keep in outbox */
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Published moisture=%d%%, msg_id=%d",
             moisture_pct, msg_id);
}
```

The function uses `esp_mqtt_client_enqueue()`, not `esp_mqtt_client_publish()`. Publish sends in the task that calls it and can block for up to 10 seconds on a slow network. These functions are called from a timer callback (below), and a timer callback must never block: one esp_timer task runs the timer callbacks of the whole system, including those of other drivers, and a blocked callback delays them all. Enqueue puts the message in the client's outbox (the last argument, `true`, keeps it there until it is sent), and the MQTT task sends it. Both return a message ID, or a negative value on failure. A message ID means only that the client accepted the message for sending: not that the broker has it, and nothing about any subscriber. The last parameter is the retain flag. Readings are not retained here, because an old reading shown as new would mislead.

### Alert Publishing

When the moisture falls below the threshold, the device publishes an alert on its own topic with QoS 2, so the broker receives it once. Whether your phone shows it depends on the phone: it must be connected and subscribed, or have a persistent session with QoS 1 or 2.

```c
static void publish_alert(esp_mqtt_client_handle_t client,
                           int moisture_pct)
{
    char payload[128];
    snprintf(payload, sizeof(payload),
             "{\"alert\":\"low_moisture\",\"moisture\":%d,"
             "\"message\":\"Soil moisture is critically low. "
             "Water your plant!\"}",
             moisture_pct);

    esp_mqtt_client_enqueue(client,
                             "home/garden/alert",
                             payload, 0,
                             2,      /* QoS 2: exactly once */
                             0, true);
    ESP_LOGW(TAG, "ALERT: Low moisture (%d%%)", moisture_pct);
}
```

### Timer-Based Periodic Publishing

A software timer calls one function at a fixed interval, with no task of your own:

```c
static esp_mqtt_client_handle_t s_mqtt_client = NULL;
static int s_publish_interval_sec = 60;
static int s_moisture_threshold = 30;
static esp_timer_handle_t s_publish_timer;
static bool s_mqtt_connected = false;
static bool s_alert_sent = false;

static void publish_timer_callback(void *arg)
{
    if (!s_mqtt_connected) return;

    int voltage_mv = read_soil_moisture_mv();
    int moisture_pct = voltage_to_moisture_pct(voltage_mv);

    publish_moisture(s_mqtt_client, moisture_pct, voltage_mv);

    /* Send alert if below threshold (only once until recovery) */
    if (moisture_pct < s_moisture_threshold && !s_alert_sent) {
        publish_alert(s_mqtt_client, moisture_pct);
        s_alert_sent = true;
    } else if (moisture_pct >= s_moisture_threshold + 10) {
        /* Add hysteresis: recover when moisture rises 10% above threshold */
        s_alert_sent = false;
    }
}

static void start_publish_timer(void)
{
    esp_timer_create_args_t timer_args = {
        .callback = publish_timer_callback,
        .name = "mqtt_publish",
    };
    esp_timer_create(&timer_args, &s_publish_timer);
    esp_timer_start_periodic(s_publish_timer,
                              s_publish_interval_sec * 1000000ULL);
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Publish timer started, interval=%d sec",
             s_publish_interval_sec);
}
```

The `s_alert_sent` flag stops the same alert at every reading: after one alert, the next is sent only once the moisture has risen 10 points above the threshold and fallen again. With a threshold of 30, the alert is armed again at 40. Without that band, noise of a point or two near 30 would send an alert at almost every crossing.

Two limits of this firmware follow from the code:

- While the broker is not connected, the callback returns at once. Readings taken in that time are not stored and not sent later.
- The Wi-Fi handler stops after 10 quick tries. A router that is off for a few minutes leaves the monitor offline until it is restarted; the MQTT client's own reconnection cannot help without Wi-Fi. A monitor left alone for weeks needs the growing waits described in the [Wi-Fi lesson](/education/embedded-programming-esp32/wifi-network-configuration/).
- The alert is sent once. If nobody was subscribed at that moment, nobody receives it. For an alert that matters, a product repeats it until somebody confirms it, or publishes the alert state as a retained message.

## Subscribing to Commands

<InArticleAd />


The same connection also carries messages to the device. The monitor subscribes to a configuration topic, so you can change its interval and threshold with no new firmware. These are settings only: the device's own code still decides what to do with each reading, and it measures on when the network is down.

### Subscribe After Connection

Subscribe to the config topic in the handler, each time the client connects:

```c
case MQTT_EVENT_CONNECTED:
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "MQTT connected");
    esp_mqtt_client_subscribe(client, "home/garden/config", 1);
    esp_mqtt_client_publish(client, "home/garden/status",
                             "sensor-online", 0, 1, 1);
    s_mqtt_connected = true;
    break;
```

### Processing Incoming Commands

A message on a subscribed topic arrives as `MQTT_EVENT_DATA`. The topic and the data are not C strings: they come with a length and no zero byte at the end, so the code copies them first.

```c
case MQTT_EVENT_DATA: {
    /* Null-terminate the topic and data for string operations */
    char topic[64];
    char data[128];
    int topic_len = event->topic_len < 63 ? event->topic_len : 63;
    int data_len = event->data_len < 127 ? event->data_len : 127;
    memcpy(topic, event->topic, topic_len);
    topic[topic_len] = '\0';
    memcpy(data, event->data, data_len);
    data[data_len] = '\0';

    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Message on [%s]: %s", topic, data);

    if (strcmp(topic, "home/garden/config") == 0) {
        process_config_command(data);
    }
    break;
}
```

The `process_config_command` function uses cJSON to parse the incoming JSON. The expected format is:

```json
{"interval": 30, "threshold": 25}
```

Either field can be omitted, and only the provided fields are updated:

```c
#include "cJSON.h"

static void process_config_command(const char *json_str)
{
    cJSON *root = cJSON_Parse(json_str);
    if (root == NULL) {
        ESP_LOGE(TAG, "Failed to parse config JSON");
        return;
    }

    cJSON *interval = cJSON_GetObjectItem(root, "interval");
    if (cJSON_IsNumber(interval) && interval->valueint >= 5
        && interval->valueint <= 3600) {
        s_publish_interval_sec = interval->valueint;

        /* Restart the timer with the new interval */
        esp_timer_stop(s_publish_timer);
        esp_timer_start_periodic(s_publish_timer,
                                  s_publish_interval_sec * 1000000ULL);
        ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Publish interval changed to %d sec",
                 s_publish_interval_sec);
    }

    cJSON *threshold = cJSON_GetObjectItem(root, "threshold");
    if (cJSON_IsNumber(threshold) && threshold->valueint >= 0
        && threshold->valueint <= 100) {
        s_moisture_threshold = threshold->valueint;
        s_alert_sent = false;  /* Re-evaluate with new threshold */
        ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Moisture threshold changed to %d%%",
                 s_moisture_threshold);
    }

    cJSON_Delete(root);
}
```

The function checks every value before use: the interval must be 5 to 3600 seconds and the threshold 0 to 100 percent; a value outside the range is ignored. A device must never trust a command only because it arrived. When the interval changes, the timer is stopped and started again with the new period.

A command has the same problem as an alert: publishing it does not prove the device received it. The new settings live in RAM and are lost at a restart, and the serial log line is this firmware's only confirmation.

## Last Will and Testament in Detail

<InArticleAd />


The last will is part of the client configuration, so the broker receives it at the moment of connection.

### Configuration

```c
.session = {
    .last_will = {
        .topic   = "home/garden/status",
        .msg     = "sensor-offline",
        .msg_len = 14,
        .qos     = 1,
        .retain  = 1,
    },
},
```

The `retain = 1` matters. The status topic then always holds the last status, "sensor-online" or "sensor-offline", and a subscriber who arrives at any time sees it at once. Without retain, a new subscriber would know nothing until the status changed again.

### The Complete Status Flow

1. The ESP32 connects to the broker. During the CONNECT handshake, it registers the LWT: topic `home/garden/status`, message `sensor-offline`, QoS 1, retain.
2. Once connected, the ESP32 publishes `sensor-online` to `home/garden/status` with retain. This overwrites any previous retained message on that topic.
3. The sensor operates normally, publishing moisture readings.
4. If the ESP32 loses power or the network drops, the broker stops receiving packets from it. The broker waits for 1.5 times the keep-alive interval (ESP-MQTT uses 120 seconds by default, so $1.5 \times 120\,\text{s} = 180\,\text{s}$) and then treats the client as lost.
5. The broker publishes the LWT message `sensor-offline` to `home/garden/status` with retain.
6. Any subscriber on that topic receives the offline notification. New subscribers see it immediately because it is retained.
7. When the ESP32 reconnects, it publishes `sensor-online` again, overwriting the offline message.

Note the delay in step 4: for up to 3 minutes after the device has gone, the status still says "sensor-online". The status tells you that a device was alive recently, not that it is alive now.

## Complete Firmware

<InArticleAd />


This is the complete `main.c`: Wi-Fi, MQTT with TLS, the sensor, the timer, the alert, the commands and the last will.

```c
/* main.c -- Plant Soil Moisture Monitor with MQTT
 *
 * ESP-IDF v5.x project.
 * Reads a capacitive soil moisture sensor via ADC1, publishes readings
 * to a TLS-secured MQTT broker, sends alerts on low moisture, and
 * accepts configuration changes (interval, threshold) via MQTT commands.
 * Last will message notifies subscribers if the device goes offline.
 */

#include <string.h>
#include <stdlib.h>
#include <stdio.h>
#include "freertos/FreeRTOS.h"
#include "freertos/task.h"
#include "freertos/event_groups.h"
#include "esp_system.h"
#include "esp_wifi.h"
#include "esp_event.h"
#include "esp_log.h"
#include "esp_netif.h"
#include "esp_timer.h"
#include "nvs_flash.h"
#include "mqtt_client.h"
#include "esp_adc/adc_oneshot.h"
#include "esp_adc/adc_cali.h"
#include "esp_adc/adc_cali_scheme.h"
#include "cJSON.h"

/* ---------- Configuration ---------- */

static const char *TAG = "soil_mqtt";

/* Wi-Fi credentials (set via menuconfig) */
#define WIFI_SSID      CONFIG_WIFI_SSID
#define WIFI_PASSWORD  CONFIG_WIFI_PASSWORD

/* MQTT broker (set via menuconfig) */
#define MQTT_BROKER_URI   CONFIG_MQTT_BROKER_URI
#define MQTT_USERNAME     CONFIG_MQTT_USERNAME
#define MQTT_PASSWORD     CONFIG_MQTT_PASSWORD

/* MQTT topics */
#define TOPIC_MOISTURE    "home/garden/moisture"
#define TOPIC_ALERT       "home/garden/alert"
#define TOPIC_CONFIG      "home/garden/config"
#define TOPIC_STATUS      "home/garden/status"

/* ADC calibration values (adjust for your sensor) */
#define DRY_VALUE_MV      2700
#define WET_VALUE_MV      1200

/* Defaults */
#define DEFAULT_INTERVAL_SEC  60
#define DEFAULT_THRESHOLD_PCT 30
#define MAX_WIFI_RETRY        10

/* Embedded broker CA certificate */
extern const uint8_t server_cert_pem_start[]
    asm("_binary_hivemq_server_cert_pem_start");
extern const uint8_t server_cert_pem_end[]
    asm("_binary_hivemq_server_cert_pem_end");

/* ---------- Global state ---------- */

static EventGroupHandle_t s_wifi_event_group;
#define WIFI_CONNECTED_BIT  BIT0
#define WIFI_GOT_IP_BIT     BIT1

static esp_mqtt_client_handle_t s_mqtt_client = NULL;
static bool s_mqtt_connected = false;

static adc_oneshot_unit_handle_t s_adc_handle;
static adc_cali_handle_t s_cali_handle;

static esp_timer_handle_t s_publish_timer;
static int s_publish_interval_sec = DEFAULT_INTERVAL_SEC;
static int s_moisture_threshold = DEFAULT_THRESHOLD_PCT;
static bool s_alert_sent = false;

static int s_wifi_retry_count = 0;

/* ---------- ADC initialization ---------- */

static void adc_init(void)
{
    adc_oneshot_unit_init_cfg_t unit_cfg = {
        .unit_id = ADC_UNIT_1,
    };
    ESP_ERROR_CHECK(adc_oneshot_new_unit(&unit_cfg, &s_adc_handle));

    adc_oneshot_chan_cfg_t chan_cfg = {
        .atten = ADC_ATTEN_DB_12,
        .bitwidth = ADC_BITWIDTH_12,
    };
    ESP_ERROR_CHECK(adc_oneshot_config_channel(s_adc_handle,
                                                ADC_CHANNEL_0, &chan_cfg));

#if ADC_CALI_SCHEME_CURVE_FITTING_SUPPORTED
    adc_cali_curve_fitting_config_t cali_cfg = {
        .unit_id = ADC_UNIT_1,
        .chan = ADC_CHANNEL_0,
        .atten = ADC_ATTEN_DB_12,
        .bitwidth = ADC_BITWIDTH_12,
    };
    ESP_ERROR_CHECK(adc_cali_create_scheme_curve_fitting(&cali_cfg,
                                                          &s_cali_handle));
#elif ADC_CALI_SCHEME_LINE_FITTING_SUPPORTED
    adc_cali_line_fitting_config_t cali_cfg = {
        .unit_id = ADC_UNIT_1,
        .atten = ADC_ATTEN_DB_12,
        .bitwidth = ADC_BITWIDTH_12,
    };
    ESP_ERROR_CHECK(adc_cali_create_scheme_line_fitting(&cali_cfg,
                                                         &s_cali_handle));
#endif

    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "ADC1 channel 0 (GPIO 36) initialized");
}

static int read_soil_moisture_mv(void)
{
    int raw = 0;
    int voltage_mv = 0;

    adc_oneshot_read(s_adc_handle, ADC_CHANNEL_0, &raw);
    adc_cali_raw_to_voltage(s_cali_handle, raw, &voltage_mv);

    return voltage_mv;
}

static int voltage_to_moisture_pct(int voltage_mv)
{
    if (voltage_mv >= DRY_VALUE_MV) return 0;
    if (voltage_mv <= WET_VALUE_MV) return 100;

    return (DRY_VALUE_MV - voltage_mv) * 100
           / (DRY_VALUE_MV - WET_VALUE_MV);
}

/* ---------- MQTT publish helpers ---------- */

static void publish_moisture(int moisture_pct, int voltage_mv)
{
    char payload[128];
    snprintf(payload, sizeof(payload),
             "{\"moisture\":%d,\"voltage_mv\":%d,\"uptime\":%lld}",
             moisture_pct, voltage_mv,
             (long long)(esp_timer_get_time() / 1000000));

    /* enqueue, not publish: this runs in a timer callback, which must not block */
    int msg_id = esp_mqtt_client_enqueue(s_mqtt_client,
                                          TOPIC_MOISTURE,
                                          payload, 0,
                                          1, 0, true);
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Published: moisture=%d%%, voltage=%d mV, msg_id=%d",
             moisture_pct, voltage_mv, msg_id);
}

static void publish_alert(int moisture_pct)
{
    char payload[128];
    snprintf(payload, sizeof(payload),
             "{\"alert\":\"low_moisture\",\"moisture\":%d,"
             "\"message\":\"Soil moisture is critically low. "
             "Water your plant!\"}",
             moisture_pct);

    esp_mqtt_client_enqueue(s_mqtt_client, TOPIC_ALERT,
                             payload, 0, 2, 0, true);
    ESP_LOGW(TAG, "ALERT: Low moisture (%d%%)", moisture_pct);
}

/* ---------- Publish timer callback ---------- */

static void publish_timer_callback(void *arg)
{
    if (!s_mqtt_connected) return;

    int voltage_mv = read_soil_moisture_mv();
    int moisture_pct = voltage_to_moisture_pct(voltage_mv);

    publish_moisture(moisture_pct, voltage_mv);

    if (moisture_pct < s_moisture_threshold && !s_alert_sent) {
        publish_alert(moisture_pct);
        s_alert_sent = true;
    } else if (moisture_pct >= s_moisture_threshold + 10) {
        s_alert_sent = false;
    }
}

static void start_publish_timer(void)
{
    esp_timer_create_args_t timer_args = {
        .callback = publish_timer_callback,
        .name = "mqtt_publish",
    };
    esp_timer_create(&timer_args, &s_publish_timer);
    esp_timer_start_periodic(s_publish_timer,
                              (uint64_t)s_publish_interval_sec * 1000000ULL);
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Publish timer started, interval=%d sec",
             s_publish_interval_sec);
}

/* ---------- Config command processing ---------- */

static void process_config_command(const char *json_str)
{
    cJSON *root = cJSON_Parse(json_str);
    if (root == NULL) {
        ESP_LOGE(TAG, "Failed to parse config JSON");
        return;
    }

    cJSON *interval = cJSON_GetObjectItem(root, "interval");
    if (cJSON_IsNumber(interval) && interval->valueint >= 5
        && interval->valueint <= 3600) {
        s_publish_interval_sec = interval->valueint;
        esp_timer_stop(s_publish_timer);
        esp_timer_start_periodic(s_publish_timer,
                                  (uint64_t)s_publish_interval_sec * 1000000ULL);
        ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Publish interval changed to %d sec",
                 s_publish_interval_sec);
    }

    cJSON *threshold = cJSON_GetObjectItem(root, "threshold");
    if (cJSON_IsNumber(threshold) && threshold->valueint >= 0
        && threshold->valueint <= 100) {
        s_moisture_threshold = threshold->valueint;
        s_alert_sent = false;
        ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Moisture threshold changed to %d%%",
                 s_moisture_threshold);
    }

    cJSON_Delete(root);
}

/* ---------- MQTT event handler ---------- */

static void mqtt_event_handler(void *handler_args,
                                 esp_event_base_t base,
                                 int32_t event_id,
                                 void *event_data)
{
    esp_mqtt_event_handle_t event = event_data;

    switch ((esp_mqtt_event_id_t)event_id) {
        case MQTT_EVENT_CONNECTED:
            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "MQTT connected to broker");
            s_mqtt_connected = true;

            /* Publish online status with retain */
            esp_mqtt_client_publish(s_mqtt_client, TOPIC_STATUS,
                                     "sensor-online", 0, 1, 1);

            /* Subscribe to configuration commands */
            esp_mqtt_client_subscribe(s_mqtt_client, TOPIC_CONFIG, 1);
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_DISCONNECTED:
            ESP_LOGW(TAG, "MQTT disconnected");
            s_mqtt_connected = false;
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_SUBSCRIBED:
            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Subscribed to topic, msg_id=%d",
                     event->msg_id);
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_PUBLISHED:
            ESP_LOGD(TAG, "Message published, msg_id=%d",
                     event->msg_id);
            break;

        case MQTT_EVENT_DATA: {
            char topic[64];
            char data[256];
            int tlen = event->topic_len < 63 ? event->topic_len : 63;
            int dlen = event->data_len < 255 ? event->data_len : 255;
            memcpy(topic, event->topic, tlen);
            topic[tlen] = '\0';
            memcpy(data, event->data, dlen);
            data[dlen] = '\0';

            ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Received [%s]: %s", topic, data);

            if (strcmp(topic, TOPIC_CONFIG) == 0) {
                process_config_command(data);
            }
            break;
        }

        case MQTT_EVENT_ERROR:
            ESP_LOGE(TAG, "MQTT error, type=%d",
                     event->error_handle->error_type);
            if (event->error_handle->error_type ==
                MQTT_ERROR_TYPE_TCP_TRANSPORT) {
                ESP_LOGE(TAG, "TLS error code: 0x%04x",
                         event->error_handle->esp_tls_last_esp_err);
                ESP_LOGE(TAG, "TLS stack error: 0x%04x",
                         event->error_handle->esp_tls_stack_err);
            }
            break;

        default:
            break;
    }
}

/* ---------- MQTT initialization ---------- */

static void mqtt_init(void)
{
    esp_mqtt_client_config_t mqtt_cfg = {
        .broker = {
            .address = {
                .uri = MQTT_BROKER_URI,
            },
            .verification = {
                .certificate = (const char *)server_cert_pem_start,
            },
        },
        .credentials = {
            .username = MQTT_USERNAME,
            .authentication = {
                .password = MQTT_PASSWORD,
            },
            .client_id = "esp32-soil-monitor",
        },
        .session = {
            .last_will = {
                .topic = TOPIC_STATUS,
                .msg = "sensor-offline",
                .msg_len = 14,
                .qos = 1,
                .retain = 1,
            },
        },
    };

    s_mqtt_client = esp_mqtt_client_init(&mqtt_cfg);
    esp_mqtt_client_register_event(s_mqtt_client, ESP_EVENT_ANY_ID,
                                     mqtt_event_handler, NULL);
    esp_mqtt_client_start(s_mqtt_client);

    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "MQTT client started, connecting to %s",
             MQTT_BROKER_URI);
}

/* ---------- Wi-Fi event handler ---------- */

static void wifi_event_handler(void *arg, esp_event_base_t event_base,
                                int32_t event_id, void *event_data)
{
    if (event_base == WIFI_EVENT) {
        switch (event_id) {
            case WIFI_EVENT_STA_START:
                esp_wifi_connect();
                break;

            case WIFI_EVENT_STA_DISCONNECTED:
                s_mqtt_connected = false;
                if (s_wifi_retry_count < MAX_WIFI_RETRY) {
                    esp_wifi_connect();
                    s_wifi_retry_count++;
                    ESP_LOGW(TAG, "Wi-Fi reconnect attempt %d/%d",
                             s_wifi_retry_count, MAX_WIFI_RETRY);
                } else {
                    ESP_LOGE(TAG, "Wi-Fi max retries reached");
                    xEventGroupClearBits(s_wifi_event_group,
                                         WIFI_CONNECTED_BIT |
                                         WIFI_GOT_IP_BIT);
                }
                break;

            default:
                break;
        }
    }

    if (event_base == IP_EVENT && event_id == IP_EVENT_STA_GOT_IP) {
        ip_event_got_ip_t *evt = (ip_event_got_ip_t *)event_data;
        ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Got IP: " IPSTR, IP2STR(&evt->ip_info.ip));
        s_wifi_retry_count = 0;
        xEventGroupSetBits(s_wifi_event_group, WIFI_GOT_IP_BIT);
    }
}

/* ---------- Wi-Fi initialization ---------- */

static void wifi_init(void)
{
    s_wifi_event_group = xEventGroupCreate();

    esp_netif_init();
    esp_event_loop_create_default();
    esp_netif_create_default_wifi_sta();

    wifi_init_config_t cfg = WIFI_INIT_CONFIG_DEFAULT();
    esp_wifi_init(&cfg);

    esp_event_handler_instance_register(WIFI_EVENT, ESP_EVENT_ANY_ID,
                                         &wifi_event_handler, NULL, NULL);
    esp_event_handler_instance_register(IP_EVENT, IP_EVENT_STA_GOT_IP,
                                         &wifi_event_handler, NULL, NULL);

    wifi_config_t wifi_config = {
        .sta = {
            .ssid = WIFI_SSID,
            .password = WIFI_PASSWORD,
            .threshold.authmode = WIFI_AUTH_WPA2_PSK,
        },
    };
    esp_wifi_set_mode(WIFI_MODE_STA);
    esp_wifi_set_config(WIFI_IF_STA, &wifi_config);
    esp_wifi_start();

    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Wi-Fi initialized, connecting to %s", WIFI_SSID);

    /* Wait for IP address before starting MQTT */
    xEventGroupWaitBits(s_wifi_event_group, WIFI_GOT_IP_BIT,
                        false, true, portMAX_DELAY);
}

/* ---------- Entry point ---------- */

void app_main(void)
{
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Plant Soil Moisture Monitor starting");

    /* Initialize NVS */
    esp_err_t ret = nvs_flash_init();
    if (ret == ESP_ERR_NVS_NO_FREE_PAGES ||
        ret == ESP_ERR_NVS_NEW_VERSION_FOUND) {
        nvs_flash_erase();
        nvs_flash_init();
    }

    /* Initialize the ADC for soil moisture reading */
    adc_init();

    /* Connect to Wi-Fi (blocks until IP is obtained) */
    wifi_init();

    /* Start the MQTT client */
    mqtt_init();

    /* Start the periodic publish timer */
    start_publish_timer();

    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Initialization complete");
    ESP_LOGI(TAG, "Publishing every %d sec, threshold=%d%%",
             s_publish_interval_sec, s_moisture_threshold);
}
```

### How the Firmware Works

The firmware has five parts:

1. The ADC part sets up ADC1 channel 0 (GPIO 36) with 12 dB attenuation and line-fitting calibration (the scheme the ESP32 supports; the same code selects curve fitting on newer chips). `read_soil_moisture_mv()` returns millivolts and `voltage_to_moisture_pct()` turns them into 0 to 100 percent.

2. The Wi-Fi part joins the network as a station and reconnects up to 10 times. `wifi_init()` waits for an IP address, so MQTT starts only when the network is ready.

3. The MQTT part sets up TLS, the broker login and the last will. At each connection it publishes "sensor-online" (retained) and subscribes to the config topic.

4. The timer runs every `s_publish_interval_sec` seconds. It reads the sensor, puts the reading in the outbox with QoS 1 for the MQTT task to send, and decides whether to send the alert with QoS 2.

5. The config part parses the JSON from the config topic, checks `interval` and `threshold`, and restarts the timer.

## Testing with MQTT Explorer

<InArticleAd />


The serial log shows what the device sent. To see what the broker received, connect a second client. MQTT Explorer is a free desktop program that shows every message on a broker as it arrives.

<Steps>
1. Download MQTT Explorer from [mqtt-explorer.com](http://mqtt-explorer.com/) (available for Windows, macOS, and Linux).

2. Open the application and create a new connection:
   - **Name:** HiveMQ Cloud
   - **Host:** your cluster URL (e.g., `xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx.s1.eu.hivemq.cloud`)
   - **Port:** 8883
   - **Protocol:** mqtt://
   - **Username:** the same credentials you created earlier
   - **Password:** the same password
   - Turn on the **Encryption (tls)** switch (the app does not enable TLS by itself for port 8883)

3. Click **Connect**. The left panel shows a tree of all topics on the broker.

4. Expand `home` then `garden`. You should see:
   - `moisture`: JSON payloads arriving at your configured interval
   - `status`: shows "sensor-online" (retained message)
   - `config`: empty until you publish a command

5. To test remote configuration, click on the **Publish** section at the bottom. Set the topic to `home/garden/config` and enter this payload:
   ```json
   {"interval": 10, "threshold": 50}
   ```
   Click Publish. The ESP32 serial monitor should log the config change, and you will see messages arriving every 10 seconds instead of 60.

6. To test the last will, unplug the ESP32 USB cable. After the broker's keep-alive timeout expires (1.5 times the keep-alive interval, so about 180 seconds with the ESP-MQTT default of 120 seconds), the `home/garden/status` topic changes from "sensor-online" to "sensor-offline".
</Steps>

### Check it

A message on the broker proves the link, not the measurement. Check both:

1. **The voltage.** Measure GPIO 36 to GND with a multimeter while the log prints a reading. The two should agree within about 50 mV between 150 and 2450 mV, the range in which the ADC is specified. A larger difference means the calibration did not load; a reading of 0 mV with a real voltage on the pin means the conversion failed.
2. **The percentage.** Put the measured millivolts into the formula by hand: 1770 mV must give 62 percent with the end points of this lesson. Then water the pot: the reading must rise within one interval and fall back slowly over the following days.
3. **The count.** In MQTT Explorer, count the readings that arrive in 10 minutes at the 60 second interval: 10. Fewer means readings were lost while the client was not connected (the first limit listed under periodic publishing). Time the last will too: from the moment you unplug, it arrives within about 1 to 3 minutes, depending on when the device last sent a packet.

### Alternative: Phone MQTT Apps

To test from a phone, install a free MQTT app: IoT MQTT Panel or MQTT Dashboard on Android, MQTTool or EasyMQTT on iOS. Connect to the same broker with the same login, subscribe to `home/garden/#` to see every message of the sensor, and publish to `home/garden/config` to change the settings. In IoT MQTT Panel you can add a gauge for `home/garden/moisture` that reads the `moisture` field.

:::tip[Instrument it]
Publish the value that matters (a temperature, a vibration level, an event) and not every raw sample. Give topics a clear order such as `site/machine/metric`, retain the last status so that a new client sees it at once, and set a last will so that a missing device is noticed. Send these messages to a broker you run, or to [siliconwit.io](https://siliconwit.io), which follows the same pattern: telemetry on `d/<id>/t`, commands on `d/<id>/c`. Either one is for records, alerts and comparing sites. Limits that protect a machine stay in the device's own firmware.
:::

## Exercises

<InArticleAd />


1. **Add multiple sensor zones.** Support two soil moisture sensors on two ADC channels (for example GPIO 36 and GPIO 39). Publish each to its own topic, `home/garden/zone1/moisture` and `home/garden/zone2/moisture`, with zone-specific alert and config topics and separate thresholds and alert states.

2. **Implement a persistent session with offline message queuing.** Use `clean_session = false` (`.session.disable_clean_session = true`) and a fixed client ID. In the phone app, subscribe to `home/garden/moisture` with QoS 1, disconnect, wait several publish cycles, and reconnect. Check that every message published while it was offline arrives. The broker queues messages only for QoS 1 and QoS 2 subscriptions, not QoS 0.

3. **Build a moving average filter for ADC readings.** Raw ADC values can be noisy, especially as soil conditions change. Average the last 10 readings, kept in a ring buffer, and publish the result, with a `"raw_mv"` field beside it so you can compare the two in MQTT Explorer.

4. **Add a local OLED display.** Connect a 128x64 I2C OLED (SSD1306) and show the moisture percentage, the MQTT status ("Online" or "Offline"), the publish interval and the threshold. Update it at each reading and each config change. The device is then useful with no phone nearby.

## Summary

<InArticleAd />


- MQTT devices connect outward to a broker. They publish to topics and subscribe to topics, and never talk to each other directly.
- QoS 0, 1 and 2 give at most once, at least once and exactly once, for one step of the journey. Published does not mean received by a subscriber.
- A retained message is the last value of a topic. A persistent session stores messages for a client that is away. A last will reports a device that disappeared, after up to 1.5 times the keep-alive.
- TLS encrypts the traffic, and the root CA certificate in the firmware lets the device check that the broker is genuine.
- The moisture percentage is a position between your measured dry and wet voltages.
- Commands change settings. Check every value, and keep the decisions in the device.
